Four tables and a window full of fog.

The room seats twenty-two: a bench along the window, four tables, and three stools at the counter where we brew.

We light the stove at six, an hour before we open, so it's warm by the time you've parked. On most winter mornings the fog sits against the glass until about nine. If you want the bench, come early.

We roast on Mondays, five kilos at a time.

The roaster is in the back room, behind the counter. Every coffee gets its own curve, and we taste each batch on Tuesday before it goes on the board.

This is last Monday's Baba Budangiri. First crack came at 8 minutes 36 seconds, and we let it develop for another 96 seconds before dropping it at 207 °C.

Five hills and a coast.

This month's board. Each coffee comes from one named place, and we'll brew it however you like: pour-over, espresso, or South Indian filter coffee in a steel tumbler.

Prices are for one cup. Contour lines on the maps are 50 m apart, and the heavy line is the altitude the coffee grows at. Bags of 250 g are at the counter, ground to order.
